Abstract AI-drafted

Clustering is a workhorse of unsupervised learning, yet classical objectives optimize only compactness and can entrench demographic disparities. This survey gives an algorithmic account of fair clustering, organized around where fairness is imposed. Part I focuses on group fairness — balanced representation within clusters, demographic constraints on the chosen centers, and min-max (socially fair) cost — and reviews the approximation guarantees, hardness results, and core techniques (fairlet decomposition, LP rounding, coresets, tree embeddings, local search) that define the area. We also summarize individual fairness, proportionality and the core, and stability-based notions, and collect representative results and open problems.
