\section{Preliminaries and Notation}\label{sec:prelim}
\aibanner

We collect the notation used throughout. An instance consists of a set of $n$ data points $P$ in a metric space $(X,d)$, where $d$ obeys the triangle inequality. A \emph{clustering} partitions $P$ into $k$ groups $\mathcal{C}=\{C_1,\dots,C_k\}$; in \emph{centroid-based} formulations each cluster $C_i$ is represented by a center $c_i$ drawn from a candidate set $F\subseteq X$ (often $F=P$), and each point is assigned to its nearest open center.

\paragraph{Clustering objectives.} The three canonical objectives are unified by the $\ell_p$ cost of a center set $C$, namely $\mathrm{cost}_p(P,C)=\big(\sum_{x\in P} d(x,C)^p\big)^{1/p}$ with $d(x,C)=\min_{c\in C} d(x,c)$. Here $p=1$ is \textbf{$k$-median}, $p=2$ is \textbf{$k$-means} (sum of squared distances), and $p\to\infty$ is \textbf{$k$-center}, which minimizes $\max_{x\in P} d(x,C)$. All three are {\bf NP}-hard, so the literature targets \emph{approximation} guarantees.

\paragraph{Protected groups.} Fairness is defined relative to $\ell$ protected groups $P_1,\dots,P_\ell\subseteq P$ induced by a sensitive attribute (e.g., race, gender, age). Groups may be \emph{disjoint} or \emph{overlapping}; we write $\Delta$ for the maximum number of groups to which any single point belongs ($\Delta=1$ in the disjoint case). For two groups it is customary to speak of \emph{red} and \emph{blue} points.

\paragraph{Approximation guarantees.} An algorithm is an $\alpha$-approximation if it returns a feasible (fair) solution of cost at most $\alpha$ times the optimum. Many fair-clustering results are instead \emph{bicriteria}: an $(\alpha,\beta)$-approximation achieves cost within $\alpha$ of optimal while relaxing the fairness requirement by a slack $\beta$ (additive or multiplicative). Tracking \emph{which} quantity is relaxed---cost or fairness---is essential when comparing results across papers.
